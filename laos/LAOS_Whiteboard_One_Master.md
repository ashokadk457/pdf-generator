## 3. Knowledge Base

### Current reusable concepts
- Long-term stuckness can coexist with high functioning.
- Over-responsibility can hide burnout.
- One trusted peer can function as a major emotional regulator for a child.
- Family financial stress can be experienced as ongoing threat.
- False-allegation stress can produce trauma-like reactions.
- Reintegration with children should be paced and child-focused.
- Shame and accountability can coexist.
- Safety, trust, and regulation often sit beneath the surface issue.

### Knowledge base rule
- Store only stable lessons that appear reusable across cases.
- Do not mix case detail with durable learning.
- If a lesson changes after later cases, update it here.
- Keep this section frozen unless the checkpoint itself is deliberately revised.

## 4. Reasoning

### What to record
- What hypothesis is being tested
- What the clinician notices first
- How the view moves from surface issue to deeper formulation
- What the reasoning path is

### Current reasoning patterns seen so far
- Start with the presenting complaint.
- Move to safety, regulation, attachment, or control.
- Test what is direct and what is inferred.
- Reframe the problem into a more workable clinical model.
- Keep the patient's own language visible.

---

## 5. Evidence

### What to record
- Direct quotations
- Timelines
- Contradictions
- Repeated statements
- Observations that support the formulation

### Evidence rule
- Separate direct evidence from inference.
- Keep quotations close to the source.
- Do not overstate certainty.

---

## 6. Confidence

### What to record
- What is high confidence
- What is moderate confidence
- What remains uncertain
- What would strengthen or weaken the view

### Confidence rule
- Confidence must be tied to evidence.
- Low certainty should stay visible.
- The report should show how sure we are and why.

---

## 7. Risk

### What to record
- Historical risk
- Current risk
- Safety concerns
- Child risk
- Relational risk
- Self-harm or passive death-wishing if present

### Risk rule
- Keep risk visible even when it is not the main topic.
- Do not collapse concern into diagnosis.
- Escalate clearly when safety is uncertain.
