## 5. What LAOS should extract from new material

Use each new transcript or case to strengthen:

- **Clinical reasoning**
  - what hypothesis is being tested
  - how the clinician moves from observation to formulation
  - what the clinician notices first

- **Evidence**
  - what in the transcript supports a conclusion
  - what is direct versus inferred
  - what should be reviewed by the clinician

- **Confidence**
  - what is certain, probable, or uncertain
  - what strengthens or weakens confidence
  - what requires further exploration

- **Risk**
  - historical risk
  - current risk
  - unresolved safety questions

- **Methodology**
  - questioning style
  - formulation style
  - therapeutic sequence
  - intervention style

- **Patient Bible**
  - patient's own stated priorities
  - recurring themes
  - longitudinal progress
  - learning statements

- **Knowledge Base**
  - reusable concepts
  - repeated patterns
  - clinician reasoning rules
  - stable reference concepts

## 6. Operating rules

### Keep separate
- AOS is not DSP-001.
- DSP-001 is not the same as Eugene.
- Intake transcripts are not consultation reasoning transcripts.
- LAOS v1 is not the LAOS Clinical Brain project.

### Preserve evidence
- Keep the patient's wording where possible.
- Distinguish patient self-report from clinician interpretation.
- Distinguish observation from inference.
- Distinguish summary from source material.

### Do not overchange
- Do not invent new framework parts unless explicitly requested.
- Do not collapse intake, reasoning, and report layers into one.
- Do not replace clinician judgment.
