# LAOS Transcript Reading Workflow

Use this one-page workflow whenever a new patient transcript arrives.

## 1. Keep the framework frozen

- LAOS v1 stays:
  - Listen
  - Assess
  - Organise
  - Support
- Do not redesign the framework.
- Use the transcript only to strengthen the support layers.

## 2. Read the transcript for the source voice

- Capture the patient’s own wording.
- Notice repeated phrases and emotional tone.
- Mark any unclear, noisy, or incomplete speech separately.
- Keep source wording distinct from interpretation.

## 3. Pull out the presenting concern

- What is the person saying is wrong?
- What is the main worry, pressure, or pain point?
- What is happening now?
- What seems to have brought the person here?

## 4. Separate evidence from inference

- Direct evidence:
  - what the transcript explicitly says
  - quotations
  - repeated statements
  - timelines
- Inference:
  - what the clinician thinks it may mean
  - any pattern or formulation that is not directly stated

## 5. Check confidence

- What is high confidence?
- What is moderate confidence?
- What is still uncertain?
- What would strengthen or weaken the view?

## 6. Check risk

- Historical risk
- Current risk
- Safety concerns
- Child risk
- Relational risk
- Passive death-wishing or self-harm language if present

## 7. Update Whiteboard One

Add or refine only:

- keywords
- patient bible

Keep the other layers in the case report or analysis note, not in Whiteboard One.

## 8. Build the patient bible

- What matters most to the person?
- What are their recurring fears, values, and needs?
- What stays stable over time?
- What changes over time?

## 9. Store reusable lessons

- What is stable enough to reuse in future cases?
- What pattern might apply to more than one transcript?
- What should stay out of the permanent knowledge base because it is too case-specific?

Only move truly stable lessons into the frozen reference set.

## 10. End with a clean summary

Write a short summary that answers:

- What happened?
- What does it mean?
- How sure are we?
- What is the risk?
- What should LAOS remember?

## Simple rule

If it is direct, keep it as evidence.
If it is interpreted, keep it as reasoning.
If it repeats, consider it for the patient bible or knowledge base.
If it changes the picture, record the confidence or risk impact.
